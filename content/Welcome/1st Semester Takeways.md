I passed. What kind of felt impossible during the semester, during the exams, and even after the exams now is black and white on my computer screen. I passed, and that’s crazy.

Here are my 15 takeaways from the first semester.

1. **Find your style of learning.** If you notice the lectures don’t get you anywhere, don’t peer-pressure yourself into having to attend them. Spend the time at the library studying with the script or the lecture recordings. I learn better when reading, and yet I spend so much time in lectures, which makes me question whether I even should. Write down all the questions you have and try to clear them as soon as possible through 1) your friends, 2) an LLM, 3) a TA, or 4) office hours (that’s been my order; vary as you wish).
2. **Focus groups are great.** There’s nothing wrong with attending a focus group if it helps you understand the topics better. Also consider going to a focus group first and then, in the same week, to a normal exercise session.
3. **Use LLMs, but learn through their output.** When using f.ex. Claude to help solve an exercise, after finishing, read through your chat history and try to actually memorize what you didn’t understand before.
4. **Time limits.** Set a three-hour window for a bonus task, get as far as you can, and do the rest with AI. Spending an entire week on one task means you don’t get to see four other tasks.
5. **Don’t give up.** During the semester I often felt like not understanding anything. During Lernphase I felt like I slowly did, but during the exams I felt like I was improvising again. Being desperate or even frustrated is a good thing. It means you’re trying to understand. As long as you generally understand what’s going on, that’s something. But accept that there are times when you don’t understand anything and times when solving the bonus tasks seems impossible. 
6. **Send emails to TAs (or even multiple TAs).** There’s something special about human responses compared to AI responses. Usually one good TA response makes you understand as much as chatting with ChatGPT in circles for an hour.
7. **Don’t over-organize, and don’t overlearn theory.** I did that for way too long. In Lernphase, quickly revise the theory through [PVW scripts](https://vis.ethz.ch/de/services/pvw-scripts/), the script, and your notes. Then practice with exercises and old exams, and clear questions along the way.
8. **Everything seems too abstract?** You simply can’t come up with proofs, you barely understand proofs? Proofs seem random, like they require a “hack” that one can’t come up with? I felt exactly the same way. High school math felt visual; this does not at all. You get used to it (even if only after the exams).
9. **You don’t have time?** I know. But you end up making it through, week by week, and at some point you end up in Lernphase. The fact is, when you’re stressed, you (or at least I do) spend two thoughts on the fact that you’re so stressed for every thought you spend on the subject itself. Just pretend you have all the time in the world and dive deep into studying.
10. **Feel warm.** I’m not going to say breaks are important because I always get annoyed when someone tells me that. Just make sure you feel warm in a sense, it's hard to describe and up to you to define.
11. **ASVZ is free and great.** I used that way too little.
12. **You earned your place.** You have done amazingly in your previous education; otherwise, you wouldn’t be here. Trust yourself that you can do this. Many people are older and seem to know everything already, but you can absolutely make it as well.
13. **Don’t compare yourself.** Many people have taken CS courses already, had *Informatik* in high school, or were coding Java since they were three years old. Give your best. Also realize that it often only seems like they know everything. Think about it in reverse, do your peers  know you’re struggling as much as you are?
14. **Not sure if CS is the right thing for you?** If it’s during the semester already, I suggest giving the exams a try. You don’t want to rule out an entire world just because the first month didn’t go as expected. Is Computer Science the right thing for me (personally)? I’m still not sure. I love technology, feel like knowing every technical product out there, love running five Codex agents at once, and think of new projects and design them in Figma. I don’t want to be a software engineer in the classical sense, but due to LLMs, coding will become agentic in the future, where vision, ideas, and creativity count more than ever. That’s what I believe – it could (for sure) also be the complete opposite.
15. **The subjects (personal).** EProg gets hard after the first timed bonus (Week 9). AuD theory stays understandable, but Code Expert seems impossible. DM is fun at first and gets very hard later. LA seems easy at first, gets very hard, and then softens again; generally less curved.

All the best!
