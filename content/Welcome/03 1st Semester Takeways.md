Here are my 15 takeaways from the first semester. Some of those might be considered "obvious" pieces of advice but that still means that even though maybe I've been told by others when I was just starting, I didn't actually follow those, and I might still be struggling to do so.

1. **Find your style of learning.** If you notice the lectures don’t get you anywhere, don’t peer-pressure yourself into having to attend them. Many people (including myself) learn much better when reading. I generally still tend to attend the lectures because it forces me to keep up with the pace of the lecture, because you get to feel how much you understand or don't. Write down all the questions you have and try to clear them as soon as possible through 1) your friends, 2) an LLM, 3) a TA, or 4) office hours (that’s been my order; vary as you wish).
2. **The subjects (my impression).** Compared to the other subjects EProg tends to seem easier in the beginning and get hard after the first timed bonus which is around week 9. Don't understimate it though; if you don't have any prior experience in coding it's very important to do the exercises to get a feeling and understanding for the thinking behind coding. If you do have coding experience, beware that often that doesn't make the exam (especially the written part) much easier. AuD theory stays understandable in Theory, but Code Expert seems impossible. Use the bonus quizzes as a reason to stay up to date with the topics, and also being paired with a random collegue was fun in my experience. DiskMat is fun at first and gets very hard later, especially since many (myself included) didn't have proofs earlier in school. LinAlg seems easy at first (although I remember not understanding the concept of hyperplanes which is like a week 1 topic), and later gets very hard, then softens again.
3. **Focus groups are great.** There’s nothing wrong with attending a focus group if it helps you understand the topics better. Also consider going to a focus group first and then, in the same week, to a normal exercise session.
4. **Use LLMs, but learn through their output.** When using f.ex. Claude to help solve an exercise, after finishing, read through your chat history and try to actually memorize what you didn’t understand before, don't use LLM's to "only" get the quick answer you need. For some period I told myself every evening to re-read all my AI conversations of that day. 
5. **The lesser you understand, the (generally) deeper you are in the topic**, which is good. If someone seems to understand if all, it might just be that they're not as deep in the material as you are. If you are unsure if you are deep enough, take a weekly exercise sheet and try to solve it as if it was the exam, meaning without AI help. It'll most likely be impossible, but now you have the picture.
6. **Time limits.** Set a two-hour (vary per task) window for a task, get as far as you can, then do the rest with external help such as with AI (this should exclude the bonus tasks obviously). Spending an entire week on one task means you don’t get to see four other tasks.
7. **Don’t give up.** During the semester I often felt like not understanding anything. During Lernphase I felt like I slowly did, but during the exams I felt like I was improvising again. Being desperate or even frustrated is a good thing. It means you’re trying to understand. As long as you generally understand what’s going on, that’s something. But accept that there are times when you don’t understand anything and times when solving the bonus tasks seems impossible. 
8. **Send emails to TAs (or even multiple TAs).** There’s something special about human responses compared to AI responses. Usually one good TA response makes you understand as much as chatting with ChatGPT in circles for an hour.
9. **Don’t over-organize, and don’t overlearn theory.** I did that for way too long. In Lernphase, quickly revise the theory through [PVW scripts](https://vis.ethz.ch/de/services/pvw-scripts/), the script, and your notes. Then practice with exercises and old exams, and clear questions along the way.
10. **Everything seems too abstract?** You simply can’t come up with proofs, you barely understand proofs? Proofs seem random, like they require a “hack” that one can’t come up with? I felt exactly the same way. High school math felt visual; this does not at all. You get used to it (even if only after the exams).
11. **You don’t have time?** I know. But you end up making it through, week by week, and at some point you end up in Lernphase. The fact is, when you’re stressed, you (or at least I do) spend two thoughts on the fact that you’re so stressed for every thought you spend on the subject itself. Just pretend you have all the time in the world and dive deep into studying.
12. **Feel warm.** I’m not going to say breaks are important because I always get annoyed when someone tells me that. Just make sure you feel warm in a sense, it's hard to describe and up to you to define.
13. **ASVZ is free and great.** I used that way too little, it's easier said than done.
14. **You earned your place.** You have done amazingly in your previous education; otherwise, you wouldn’t be here. Trust yourself that you can do this. Many people are older and seem to know everything already, but you are you and not that other person.
15. **Don’t compare yourself.** Many people have taken CS courses already, had *Informatik* in high school, or were coding Java since they were three years old. Give your best. Also realize that it often only seems like they know everything. Think about it in reverse, do your peers know you’re struggling as much as you are?
16. **Not sure if CS is the right thing for you?** If it’s during the semester already, give the exams a try. You don’t want to rule out an entire world just because the first month didn’t go as expected. Is Computer Science the right thing for me (personally)? I’m still not sure. I truly love technology, but Computer Science isn't directly that and I need creative things to keep balance. The point is, there's many of us who have to figure it out as we go.

All the best!
