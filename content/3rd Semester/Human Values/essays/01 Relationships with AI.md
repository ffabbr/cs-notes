AI has become a substantial part of my daily undertakings. In the following, I want to cover four aspects of this. Firstly, AI as a developer. Secondly, AI as a designer. Thirdly, AI as a learning companion. And fourthly, all the other daily questions.

## The use of AI as a developer

Large Language Models are better at writing code than any person on Earth. This is true now and no longer an unpopular opinion. But even years ago, I was convinced that there was no way any human on Earth could learn coding faster than AI progresses. So naturally, in conversations with people who aren't that integrated into the developer community, the question arises—What is left of a developer? That's until we realize that being a developer has never really been about writing syntax or typing on a keyboard. It's been about figuring out solutions to complex problems. And I couldn't be happier that AI models got there. I also wouldn't have started studying computer science if AI hadn't come up. It's a tool, after all. And it will always remain one.

With it, my output increases tenfold in quality.

## The use of AI as a designer

The use of AI for design work is the complete opposite of the use of AI in development. Output quality is halved whenever I try to rely extensively on AI to design.

That's because design is subjective and unverifiable. With code, you have test cases that you need to pass at the end; there is a right and a wrong answer. With design, there is not—that's the beauty. The first designer does it differently than the second designer, and vice versa. If we all rely on AI for design, we lose that touch of personality and creativity. Observation and selection are not the same as creation, and creating something from scratch is a craft that we as humanity should not lose. 

It's much easier to say what you like or dislike about a proposal given by the AI, but the end result will always be shaped by that first proposal, no matter how much you change it.

I would love to see AI as a creative partner, but currently, I don't. Which does not mean that AI isn't great at creating things and won't be a great designer one day. But it's not a creative partner.

## The use of AI as a learning companion

*I am studying engineering, and the following are my observations. Things might be different in other domains.*

Using AI as a learning companion is like having a 24/7 tutor that is not allowed to say no. That's absolutely amazing on paper. Whenever I have a question, the answer is just seconds away. I can get explanations from many different angles and in many different shapes and forms, and I use that to the fullest extent. Learning has never been easier.

But at the same time, that's the issue. Learning is not supposed to be easy. The answer itself is worthless; the hours you spend getting to that answer are where the value of learning is. The value lies in the deep thinking you do when you know you have to somehow figure out the solution to the problem yourself. And we don't do that anymore.

When you know that the solution or a hint is only seconds away, your mind grows inherently hesitant to spend hours on something you could get in seconds. I am therefore unsure what the long-term effects of using AI in learning will be.

## All the other daily questions

This is where AI really shines. We ask ourselves a million questions every day, and finding the answers with Google search takes much longer than it should. As hesitant as I am to accept AI as a guide, I love not having to click through cookie banners, different websites, ads, and more.

The answer is just right there, and you can continue with your life. This means I can live more actively and spend less time looking at a screen. Suppose I am on the tram, commuting to university, thinking about the day ahead, and a question pops up—I whisper that question into my phone, get an answer in seconds, and can continue looking out the window, thinking. I used to have to spend the rest of the commute on my phone trying to find the answer on Google.

Ultimately, AI will bring humans closer together again: technology will seem to move into the background while actually being more prevalent than ever—both at the same time.
