## Dependency

Technology has the power to change the world. This is no secret, and we've seen it happen many times before—social media, the smartphone, the computer, the internet, all the way to tractors and electricity itself. In fact, I would argue that technology is the field with the _biggest_ impact on society overall.

With great power comes even greater responsibility. Every one of the examples mentioned introduced issues that weren't there before. And while social media, the smartphone, and the internet have—without a doubt—revolutionized the way humans connect and stay in touch, in many ways humans are also more isolated than ever.

We as a society are currently developing the next revolution of this kind. I—as a rather technical person—see myself, in a very distant sense, as part of the group of people working on this technology. We want to make sure this goes right.

We want to ensure that the world is a better place after the introduction and widespread adoption of LLMs than it was before.

_But what exactly does that mean? What exactly does it mean for the world to be a better place with artificial intelligence than without it?_

That's what I'm here to figure out.

If we imagine a future scenario of people living 100 years down the road, you might think of a world where we can all simply enjoy our lives, see friends, hang out all the time, and have fun. Humans are more connected than ever and work less intensively than ever before. They hand tasks in engineering and other fields over to artificial intelligence and use the time that has been freed up to learn skateboarding at the neighborhood park. That sounds fantastic, right? 

But now, all of a sudden, we—as a society—are dependent on a system. A system developed by a company. Sold by a country.

And if we give it a hundred years of evolution in which no human does actual deep mathematical thinking—if we have 100 years of nobody solving mathematical proofs, engaging with deep engineering topics, invent fire, flight, and electricity—after those 100 years of not using that part of the brain, it will regress. This leaves us with a society that is inherently incapable of inventing fire, flight, and electricity.

Is the development of AI still a net positive in this case? You might be inclined to say no, but I, even with all of that in mind, don't know. There is no definitive answer here, which makes the debate even more interesting.

I'm hoping that this course can support me in my thinking about this topic and help me develop an understanding of what is necessary to introduce AI in a way that it aligns with human values and results in a better tomorrow for everyone.
